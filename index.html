The Vocabulary

Sentiment tools look for feeling. We look for manipulation.

Anticipation starts with knowing the playbook. Information operations have their own grammar—and FC26 is built around it, so before the pipeline scores a single post, everyone shares the same vocabulary.

Disinformation vs. Misinformation

Both are false. The difference is intent. Disinformation is engineered to deceive or cause harm; misinformation is the same falsehood shared in good faith—a voter repeating a genuine mix-up about a moved polling place. FC26 weighs the intent, not just the accuracy.

Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

A network of bots, cyborgs, or paid actors moving in lockstep to fake a groundswell—a digital choir pretending to be a crowd of random strangers.

Patient Zero

The first account or community to inject a claim into the ecosystem. Finding it is the difference between treating the symptom and tracing the virus.

Copy-Pasta Signatures

Identical blocks of text pasted across dozens of profiles inside a tight window—the fingerprint of automated or centrally-run distribution.

Narrative Mutation

How a rumor changes shape to survive. Debunk "stolen ballots" and it mutates into "the media is burying the investigation"—keeping the outrage alive.

Knowing the playbook is half the job. Catching it live—in a firehose of millions of posts a minute—is the other half. ↓ Here's how.

The Innovation

The Sequential Scoring Pipeline

Standard social listening breaks down when flooded with thousands of posts a minute. It delivers shallow word-clouds or racks up impossible computing bills—and either way, it only tells you what already happened.

FC26 solves this with a strict multi-stage blueprint—a genuinely new architecture for social intelligence. Two gatekeepers turn away obvious noise at the door (and idle the moment a claim is inert, so effort goes only to real signals). A team of specialists then works each live threat in sequence—scoring technique, credibility, coordination, and persuasion signal by signal. An adversarial "shadow" stress-tests the verdict by thinking like the attacker. Finally, a chief analyst reconciles everything into a definitive report on what's happening—and where it's headed next.

Result: Human teams stop drowning in false alarms and receive clear, multi-layered threat summaries—plus a read on the attacker's next move—exactly when they matter.

Transparent Architecture

Addressing Tough Questions

We built this system because we are skeptical of software that claims to blindly "know" what is true. Here is how we enforce objectivity.

Q: Does this just censor or flag opinions people don't agree with?

No. The system has strict, unyielding guardrails. For example, general user frustration, long lines at polling locations, complaints about weather, or political debates are protected from flags. The logic specifically searches for false, actionable operational data (e.g., falsely broadcasting that a real polling site is shut down or destroyed).

Q: How do you tell deliberate disinformation from an honest mistake?

Intent is the dividing line. A voter repeating a genuine mix-up is misinformation—messy, but not malicious—and the system treats it very differently from a coordinated network seeding the same falsehood on purpose. FC26 reads behavioral signals (timing, synchronization, copy-pasta, the Patient Zero trail) to gauge intent, and always logs its confidence and blind spots rather than asserting certainty.

Q: How do we know the AI isn't just hallucinating or making up threats?

Every single analysis is forced to go through a Phase 2 Read-Back Verification. Before an automated record can exit a bot, the platform reads its own database entries back to verify matching coherence, runs automated sanity checks on the numeric scores, and proves the data with direct snippets of source text.

Q: Who actually acts on this information?

Humans. The FC26 suite is not connected to any social network moderation controls and has no authority to delete posts or block accounts. It serves strictly as an early-warning radar dashboard, keeping human protection teams informed so they can issue verified local updates where confusion exists.
